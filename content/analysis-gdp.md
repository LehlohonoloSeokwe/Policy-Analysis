# Real GDP Growth: Structural Constraints and Output Trajectory

**Indicator**: Real Gross Domestic Product Growth (Annual %)  
**Author**: Lehlohonolo Seokwe, MA Political Science  

### 1. Trend
Following the sharp -6.0% contraction induced by the 2020 pandemic lockdowns and the subsequent 4.7% mechanical bounce-back in 2021, South Africa's real GDP growth settled into an anaemic 0.5%–1.2% band between 2022 and 2025. This rate is structurally deficient: it trails domestic population growth (~1.3% annually), meaning real per capita income has effectively stagnated for over a decade.

### 2. Why It Matters
Economic growth determines tax buoyancy, public revenue mobilization, and sovereign debt sustainability. Without real GDP growth expanding at 2.5% to 3.5%, the National Treasury cannot balance fiscal deficits without painful real expenditure cuts. Furthermore, financial institutions face compressed credit demand as corporate balance sheets defer long-term capital formation (capex) in favor of liquidity preservation.

### 3. Who Is Affected
- **Commercial Banks & Financial Institutions**: Depressed loan book expansion and higher non-performing loan (NPL) provisions in unsecured lending.
- **Young Work-Seekers**: Economic growth below 2.0% produces negative or near-zero employment elasticities, preventing formal labour absorption.
- **Sovereign Credit Rating**: Sub-1.5% medium-term potential growth caps credit rating agency assessments (Moody's, S&P, Fitch), elevating sovereign bond borrowing yields.
